# References

This page collects key literature that informs our work on narrative dynamics. We provide these references both to acknowledge the intellectual foundations of our research and to offer a starting point for those interested in exploring this field further.

## Narrative Psychology & Structure

These works address the fundamental cognitive and psychological aspects of narratives.

<div class="reference">
  <p>Bruner, J. (1991). The narrative construction of reality. <em>Critical Inquiry, 18</em>(1), 1-21.</p>
  <p class="citation">A seminal work on how narrative serves as a fundamental cognitive structure through which humans organize experience and construct reality.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/bruner-1991.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1086/448619">DOI</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Trabasso, T., & Sperry, L. L. (1985). Causal relatedness and importance of story events. <em>Journal of Memory and Language, 24</em>(5), 595-611.</p>
  <p class="citation">Introduces the concept of causal networks in narratives and how these networks influence comprehension and recall.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/trabasso-sperry-1985.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-596X(85)90048-8">DOI</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Graesser, A. C., Singer, M., & Trabasso, T. (1994). Constructing inferences during narrative text comprehension. <em>Psychological Review, 101</em>(3), 371-395.</p>
  <p class="citation">A detailed examination of how readers make causal and logical inferences during narrative comprehension.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/graesser-1994.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.101.3.371">DOI</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Mar, R. A., & Oatley, K. (2008). The function of fiction is the abstraction and simulation of social experience. <em>Perspectives on Psychological Science, 3</em>(3), 173-192.</p>
  <p class="citation">Explores how narrative fiction serves as a simulation of social worlds, allowing readers to experience emotions and social situations vicariously.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/mar-oatley-2008.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6924.2008.00073.x">DOI</a></p>
</div>

## Information Diffusion & Social Networks

These references address the spread of information through social networks.

<div class="reference">
  <p>Centola, D. (2010). The spread of behavior in an online social network experiment. <em>Science, 329</em>(5996), 1194-1197.</p>
  <p class="citation">A landmark experimental study demonstrating how network structure influences the spread of complex behaviors.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/centola-2010.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1185231">DOI</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Aral, S., & Nicolaides, C. (2017). Exercise contagion in a global social network. <em>Nature Communications, 8</em>, 14753.</p>
  <p class="citation">Investigates how social influence affects exercise behaviors, showing that influences vary based on relationship types and individual characteristics.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/aral-nicolaides-2017.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms14753">DOI</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Watts, D. J. (2002). A simple model of global cascades on random networks. <em>Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 99</em>(9), 5766-5771.</p>
  <p class="citation">Presents a threshold model for understanding how small initial shocks can trigger global cascades in complex networks.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/watts-2002.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.082090499">DOI</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Vosoughi, S., Roy, D., & Aral, S. (2018). The spread of true and false news online. <em>Science, 359</em>(6380), 1146-1151.</p>
  <p class="citation">A comprehensive analysis of how true and false news stories spread differently through social networks.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/vosoughi-2018.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aap9559">DOI</a></p>
</div>

## Computational Modeling of Narratives

These works focus on formal and computational approaches to modeling narratives.

<div class="reference">
  <p>Elson, D. K., & McKeown, K. R. (2010). Building a bank of semantically encoded narratives. <em>Proceedings of the Seventh International Conference on Language Resources and Evaluation (LREC)</em>, 2165-2172.</p>
  <p class="citation">Introduces methods for encoding narrative structures in ways that enable computational analysis.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/elson-mckeown-2010.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Chambers, N., & Jurafsky, D. (2008). Unsupervised learning of narrative event chains. <em>Proceedings of ACL-08: HLT</em>, 789-797.</p>
  <p class="citation">Presents techniques for automatically learning sequences of events (narrative schemas) from text corpora.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/chambers-jurafsky-2008.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://aclanthology.org/P08-1090/">URL</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Sack, W. (2013). The Bayesian narrational: Modeling individual differences in narrative understanding. <em>Cognitive Systems Research, 24</em>, 72-88.</p>
  <p class="citation">Explores how Bayesian inference can model narrative comprehension, accounting for individual differences.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/sack-2013.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogsys.2013.01.001">DOI</a></p>
</div>

## Collective Intelligence & Collaborative Sensemaking

These references examine how groups collectively make sense of information.

<div class="reference">
  <p>Introne, J., Laubacher, R., Olson, G., & Malone, T. (2013). The Climate CoLab: Large scale model-based collaborative planning. <em>Proceedings of the 2013 International Conference on Collaboration Technologies and Systems (CTS)</em>, 201-208.</p>
  <p class="citation">Describes a platform for collaborative problem-solving and shows how collective intelligence can be harnessed for complex issues.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/introne-2013.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1109/CTS.2013.6567228">DOI</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Klein, G., Moon, B., & Hoffman, R. R. (2006). Making sense of sensemaking 1: Alternative perspectives. <em>IEEE Intelligent Systems, 21</em>(4), 70-73.</p>
  <p class="citation">Offers a framework for understanding how people construct meaningful explanations in complex situations.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/klein-2006.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1109/MIS.2006.75">DOI</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Malone, T. W., & Bernstein, M. S. (Eds.). (2015). <em>Handbook of collective intelligence</em>. MIT Press.</p>
  <p class="citation">A comprehensive overview of research on how groups of individuals collectively solve problems and make decisions.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="https://mitpress.mit.edu/books/handbook-collective-intelligence">Publisher</a></p>
</div>

## Social Cognition & Information Processing

These works address how social context influences cognition and information processing.

<div class="reference">
  <p>Mercier, H., & Sperber, D. (2011). Why do humans reason? Arguments for an argumentative theory. <em>Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 34</em>(2), 57-74.</p>
  <p class="citation">Proposes that human reasoning evolved primarily for social argumentation rather than individual truth-seeking.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/mercier-sperber-2011.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X10000968">DOI</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Kahan, D. M. (2017). Misconceptions, misinformation, and the logic of identity-protective cognition. <em>Cultural Cognition Project Working Paper Series No. 164</em>.</p>
  <p class="citation">Explains how identity concerns can drive motivated reasoning and information processing biases.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="/assets/papers/references/kahan-2017.pdf"><i class="fas fa-file-pdf"></i> PDF</a> | <a href="https://ssrn.com/abstract=2973067">SSRN</a></p>
</div>

<div class="reference">
  <p>Sloman, S. A., & Fernbach, P. (2017). <em>The knowledge illusion: Why we never think alone</em>. Riverhead Books.</p>
  <p class="citation">Explores how human cognition is fundamentally collaborative and how we rely on communal knowledge networks.</p>
  <p class="links"><a href="https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/533524/the-knowledge-illusion-by-steven-sloman-and-philip-fernbach/">Publisher</a></p>
</div>
