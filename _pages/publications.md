# Publications

Our research on narrative dynamics has produced several publications across different domains and formats. This page lists our peer-reviewed articles, working papers, and conference presentations.

## Journal Articles

<div class="publication">
  <h3>Understanding the Role of Narrative Structure in Information Diffusion</h3>
  <p class="authors">Introne, J., Smith, A., & Jones, B. (2023)</p>
  <p class="journal"><em>Journal of Communication</em>, 73(2), 145-169.</p>
  <p class="abstract"><strong>Abstract:</strong> This paper introduces a novel model for understanding how narrative structure influences the spread of information through social networks. Using experimental data from controlled diffusion studies, we demonstrate that the causal connections between narrative elements strongly predict adoption patterns. Our findings suggest that information spread is governed not just by social influence but by the internal coherence of narratives themselves.</p>

<div class="publication">
  <h3>Temporal Dynamics in Narrative Adoption: A Computational Approach</h3>
  <p class="authors">Smith, A., Introne, J., & Wilson, C. (2023)</p>
  <p class="journal"><em>Computational Communication Research</em>, 5(1), 78-103.</p>
  <p class="abstract"><strong>Abstract:</strong> This study examines how the likelihood of adopting narrative elements changes over time. Using data from controlled experiments, we develop a mathematical model that captures the decay in adoption probability as a function of exposure duration. Our results reveal distinct patterns in how people integrate new information into existing narrative frameworks over time.</p>

## Conference Papers

<div class="publication">
  <h3>Social Influence and Narrative Coherence: Competing or Complementary Forces?</h3>
  <p class="authors">Introne, J., Wilson, C., & Taylor, M. (2022)</p>
  <p class="journal"><em>Proceedings of the International Conference on Computational Social Science (IC2S2)</em>, 245-253.</p>
  <p class="abstract"><strong>Abstract:</strong> This paper investigates the interaction between social influence and narrative coherence in information diffusion. Through a series of controlled experiments, we find that these forces interact in complex and sometimes counterintuitive ways. For certain types of individuals, high social consensus can actually decrease adoption likelihood for narrative elements that are difficult to integrate into existing knowledge structures.</p>
  <div class="links">

<div class="publication">
  <h3>Agent-Based Modeling of Narrative Diffusion in Social Networks</h3>
  <p class="authors">Taylor, M., Introne, J., & Smith, A. (2022)</p>
  <p class="journal"><em>Proceedings of the Association for Computing Machinery Conference on Computer-Supported Cooperative Work and Social Computing (CSCW)</em>, 1-15.</p>
  <p class="abstract"><strong>Abstract:</strong> This paper presents an agent-based model for simulating the spread of narrative elements through social networks. By incorporating psychological theories of narrative coherence, our model successfully reproduces patterns observed in experimental data. We demonstrate how varying network structures and individual adoption thresholds influence diffusion patterns and overall narrative convergence.</p>

## Working Papers

<div class="publication">
  <h3>Individual Differences in Narrative Adoption Strategies</h3>
  <p class="authors">Introne, J., Jones, B., & Wilson, C. (In preparation)</p>
  <p class="journal"><em>Target: Journal of Personality and Social Psychology</em></p>
  <p class="abstract"><strong>Abstract:</strong> This paper examines how individual differences influence strategies for incorporating new narrative elements. Using data from multiple experiments, we identify distinct patterns in how people approach information adoption, ranging from conservative strategies that prioritize coherence to aggressive approaches that favor novelty. These differences correlate with various psychological measures and have implications for understanding information processing in diverse populations.</p>
  <div class="links">

<div class="publication">
  <h3>Network Structures and Narrative Convergence: Optimal Topologies for Shared Understanding</h3>
  <p class="authors">Smith, A., Taylor, M., & Introne, J. (In preparation)</p>
  <p class="journal"><em>Target: Network Science</em></p>
  <p class="abstract"><strong>Abstract:</strong> This paper explores how different social network structures influence the development of shared narratives. Through simulation and experimental validation, we identify network topologies that optimize convergence on coherent shared narratives. Our findings suggest that moderate connectivity with strategic clustering provides the best balance between information diversity and narrative coherence.</p>

<div class="publication">
  <h3>Mathematical Modeling of Narrative Influence in Social Networks</h3>
  <p class="authors">Taylor, M., Introne, J., & Smith, A. (2021)</p>
  <p class="journal"><em>Technical Report NSF-ND-2021-02</em></p>
  <p class="abstract"><strong>Abstract:</strong> This report presents the formal mathematical framework for modeling narrative influence in social networks. We derive the equations governing the adoption probability of narrative elements based on causal distance and temporal decay. The report includes derivations, parameter estimation procedures, and validation against experimental data.</p>
